# Backseat Driver privacy policy

_Last updated: November 1, 2026_

Backseat Driver is a navigation app with a sarcastic voice. It's built on CoMaps, an open-source offline maps app. This page explains what the app does with your data. In short, it keeps it on your phone.

## What stays on your phone

- **Your location.** The app uses GPS to give directions and comment on your driving. Your location, routes and trips are kept on your device and aren't sent to us.
- **Your voice.** When you talk to the app, speech is turned into text by Apple's speech recognition. On iPhones that support it, this happens on the device. Audio isn't recorded or kept.
- **The app's voice and jokes.** The spoken voice (Kokoro) and the rewritten lines (Apple's on-device model) run on your phone.
- **Settings, trip history and mileage log.** These are stored on your device. If you share them yourself, for example by exporting the drive log, you choose where they go.

## What leaves your phone

- **Map downloads.** Map data is downloaded from CoMaps' map servers. Those servers see your IP address and which map regions you download, but not your location.
- **Weather.** To make weather jokes, the app sends your approximate coordinates to a free public weather service: the US National Weather Service (api.weather.gov) in the US, or MET Norway (api.met.no) elsewhere. No account or identifier is sent.
- **Crash reports.** If you agree to share analytics with app developers in iOS settings, Apple may pass along anonymous crash reports.

## What we don't do

- No ads, no tracking, no analytics SDKs, no accounts.
- We don't sell or share your data.
- We don't collect your name, email or contacts.

## TestFlight

If you're testing through TestFlight, Apple collects some information under its own TestFlight terms, such as your email, device model and crash logs. Apple shares that with the developer.
